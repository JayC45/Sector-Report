What is a Drone?

A drone simply refers to an unmanned vehicle, which includes land rovers, submarines, and aircraft. However, the term is most commonly used to refer to unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs), including quadcopters and planes, which is what this page is about.

Current Trends in Drone Technology

⦿ The drone industry is growing rapidly
⦿ UAVs will soon become much more common, including everyday uses such as delivery or photography all the way up to military applications
⦿ AI integration is currently the biggest driver of innovation in the sector for its use in autonomous flight and automatic decision-making

⦿ The DroneX conference in Europe showcased that AI is being used to create increasingly powerful and intelligent drones.
⦿ As autonomy increases, cybersecurity become more critical to ensure that drones are not misused or hacked.
⦿ Stonger regulations will also be needed to control airspace and avoid crashes between the increasing number of drones

Parts of a drone

Airframe / Body

⦿ Smaller drones are often made with injection molded plastic, though some can be 3D printed.
⦿ Higher performance drones tend to be made out of carbon fiber composite structures to withstand the greater forces and stresses of high speed flight.
⦿ The largest drones are often made out of aluminium alloys or titanium alloys to provide a strong and lightweight structure.

Propulsion

⦿ Electric brushless motors are the most common type of propulsion for drones which give efficiency and control.
⦿ Some drones use internal combustion engines for longer flight times and greater payload capacity.
⦿ Both types of propulsion use propellers to generate thrust. These can be made out of plastic, carbon fiber, or wood.

Emerging Propulsion Technologies

⦿ There are new types of propulsion being being introduced to the sector.
⦿ EFPL is working on acoustic jets that blast high frequency sound waves to produce thrust.
⦿ Many companies are working on ionic thrusters which don't need moving parts.
⦿ Perhaps the most promising development is toroidal propellers. These allow for quieter and more efficient flight compared to traditional propellers.

Flight controllers (FCs)

⦿ Flight controllers are essentially the brain of the drone, managing its controls to direct the drone to its desired location.
⦿ FCs include a microprocessor (a small computer) along with many sensors.
⦿ Accelerometers and gyroscopes are required to detect the drone's orientation.
⦿ Other sensors may be used to gather information about the drone's environment and performance.

Navigation

⦿ Navigation systems included on certain drones to guide them along their intended path.
⦿ GPS is the most common method for positioning which connects directly to the flight controller.
⦿ The flight controller takes the GPS data along with its own sensor data to navigate along the intended path.
⦿ GPS works by receiving signals from satellites orbiting the Earth to give accurate position information.

Payload

⦿ The payload is the equipment or cargo that the drone is designed to carry.
⦿ Payload capacity varies depending on the drone's size and design.
⦿ Common payloads include cameras, sensors, and delivery items.
⦿ Usually, the drone is designed around the specific payload it is designed to carry.

How to Build a Career in the Drone Industry

Drones aren't a novelty anymore, they're everyday tools for almost everybody: farmers, survey crews, utility companies and construction managers. Clients and hiring managers don't care if you can fly manual maneuvers, they care if you can deliver accurate usable data without violating air spaces.

What to Know Before Pursuing a Commercial Drone Career

Get your advanced certificate right away

A basic certificate limits you to open spaces far from people and airports. Almost every paying job in Canada, from site progress photos to utility inspections, happens near people or inside controlled airspace.

The ideal path would require taking a recognized ground school, passing Transport Canada's advanced exam, and booking a flight review. You should also get your ROC-A (Aeronautical Radio Operator certificate) which certifies you to communicate with air traffic controllers when working in controlled airspace.

Master the data not just the controller

Flying the aircraft is just a small part of the job. The real work is turning raw images and sensor logs into CAD files, spreadsheets or 3D models.

  • You should learn a variety of processing platforms.
  • You should also pick a technical specialty such as thermal imaging, LiDAR for elevation mapping or multi spectral sensors for crop health.

Show workforce competence rather than photography skills.

A cute collage of scenic sunset photos isn't what employers are usually looking for, show proof that you understand commercial photography and programming.

  • Include 3D models and proof of your technical specialty in accordance with the sector you're applying for.
  • Include proof you're competent in your specialty using past jobs in the industry and samples from your drone work. Be sure to show your various authorization records to prove you're a strong candidate.

Target sectors who mean business

Don't chase low-margin drone photography and focus on industries with recurring commercial contracts.

  • Utilities and energy
  • Construction and geomatics
  • Agriculture and forestry
  • Remote logistics
